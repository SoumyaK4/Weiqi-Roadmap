## Beginner

### Free

- **Falling In Love With Baduk - Play a Game in One Week (by KBA)** → official Korean Baduk Association guide <a href="https://senseis.xmp.net/?FallingInLoveWithBaduk" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Download</a>
- **A Go Guide from a Beginner (by Charalampos Kapolos)** → available in many languages <a href="https://www.gobook.eu/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Download</a>
- **Improving at Go (by Jonathan Prevost)** → not a technical Go book, but one about what to expect on your journey <a href="https://improve.tenuki.net/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer">Download</a>

### Recommended books

1. **Single introductory book:**
   - **Go: A Complete Introduction to the Game (by Cho Chikun)**
   - **A Scientific Introduction to Go (by Yang Yu-Chia)**
   - **Learn Go: Possibly the most played board game in the World (by Neil Moffatt)**

2. **First volume of one of these beloved book series:**
   - **So You Want to Play Go? - Level 1 (30 kyu - 20 kyu) (by Jonathan Hop)**
   - **Learn to Play Go: A Master's Guide to the Ultimate Game (Volume I) (by Janice Kim, Soo-hyun Jeong)**

There are many good books for beginners, but some I find hard for real beginners (e.g. **Second Book of Go by Richard Bozulich**), a bit confusing for real beginners (e.g. **Shapes of mind - Go course for beginners by Mateusz Surma**) or a bit too dense for real beginners (e.g. **Go for Beginners by Kaoru Iwamoto**). These are wonderful books and should be read, but not as the first initiation text.

## SDK

The SDK book list is an extension of the DDK book list. Some of the books in the later are worth reading and re-reading at multiple milestones until achieving high Dan (e.g. Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go) and some strong players recommend exactly this. Nevertheless, some concepts and ideas can flourish only after achieving better familiarity and understanding of the game. Consequently, the topics addressed by the best SDK books become quite diverse. Here is only a narrower-scoped selection of what I consider the most useful books.

### Opening & Joseki
- Fuseki Revolution: How AI Has Changed Go (by Toramaru Shibano)
- Joseki Revolution: Overthrowing Conventional Wisdom (by Toramaru Shibano)
- Typical Mistake (by Young Sun Yoon)
- Whole Board Thinking in Joseki Vol. 2 (by Yilun Yang)

### Attack & Defense
- Attack and Defence (by James Davies)
- Improve Fast In Go (by Milton N. Bradley)
- Attacking and Defending weak groups (Rob van Zeijst & Richard Bozulich)

### End Game
- The End Game (by Tomoko Ogawa)
- Absolute Counting: in the Endgame (by Meien O)
- Micro endgame of Go (by hanayeol)

### Proverbs / Principles
- Essential Go Proverbs (by John Power)

### Fundamentals
- The Fundamental Principles of Go (by Yilun Yang)
- The Direction of Play (by Takeo Kajiwara)

### Theory
- EZ-Go: Oriental Strategy in a Nutshell (by Bruce Wilcox)
- Counting Liberties and Winning Capturing Races (by Richard Hunter)

### L&D Theory
- Tsume-Go Strategy 1 Learn to Recognize Vital Points in Go Problems — Corner Patterns (by Thomas Redecker)
- Mastering basic corner shapes Step-by-step (by Young Sun Yoon)

### Tesuji
- Get Strong at Tesuji (by Richard Bozulich)
- Tesuji and Anti-Suji of Go (by Sakata Eio)

### Games
- Understanding Dan Level Play (by Yuan Zhou)
- Master Play: The Style of Lee Changho (by Yuan Zhou)
- Master Play: The Style of Takemiya (by Yuan Zhou)
- Master Play - The Style of Go Seigen (by Yuan Zhou)
- Invincible - The Games of Shusaku (by John Power)
